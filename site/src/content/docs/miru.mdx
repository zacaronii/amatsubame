Miru plays the videos on your own [Jellyfin](https://jellyfin.org) server through mpv, so it handles
the files anime fansubs and Blu-ray rips come in. Japanese subtitles are drawn by Miru itself: tap any
word for the dictionary popup, and mine it to Anki with a picture and the line's audio. Every line you
watch is kept in **Lines**, where you can read and search it later.

## What you need

- A Jellyfin server and a user account on it.
- Japanese subtitles: text subtitles in your Jellyfin library (SRT, ASS and the like, separate or
  inside the video), or a free [Jimaku](https://jimaku.cc) account to find them, or
  [Kaku](/kaku/) to make them from the audio.
- Optional, for mining: Anki with AnkiConnect. See [Lookup and Anki](/lookup-and-anki/).

## Connecting Jellyfin

The first time you open Miru, setup asks for your server address (for example
`https://jellyfin.example.com`), your username and your password. The password is only used to sign
in: Miru keeps a sign-in token, never the password. Then it shows your Jellyfin libraries and asks
which ones are Japanese. [Getting started](/getting-started/) covers the rest of setup.

Later you'll find the server in **Settings → Jellyfin** (with **Sign Out** and **Test Connection**),
and the libraries in **Settings → Libraries**.

## Browsing

Miru has four tabs: **Watch**, **Lines**, **Stats** and **Settings**.

- **Watch** opens with **Continue Watching**, **Next Up**, what's **On This Device**, and each
  library's recently added titles. Search finds shows and movies by name.
- A show's page lists its episodes, with a filter: **All**, **Unwatched** or **Downloaded**.
- **Japanese audio only:** in **Settings → Playback**, turn this on for a library to hide movies and
  shows without a Japanese audio track. Episodes with several audio tracks always start on the
  Japanese one.
- On iPad and Mac, your libraries are also listed in the sidebar under the tabs.
- On the Mac, a library's filter is in the toolbar: **All**, **Watching**, **Unwatched** or
  **Watched**, with a **Sort** menu (**Title**, **Recently Added**, **Recently Watched**). Downloads
  are in the sidebar under **On This Mac ▸ Downloaded**.
- On the Mac, **Pin** in a show or movie's toolbar adds it to the sidebar's **Pinned** section.
  Right-click a pinned one and choose **Unpin** to take it out.

## Watching

Playback picks up where you left off, here or on any other Jellyfin app.

On iPhone and iPad:

- Tap to show the controls, or tap with two fingers to pause without them.
- Double-tap the left or right side to skip back or forward.
- Drag sideways to scrub, with a preview of the picture.
- Drag up or down along an edge to change the brightness or volume.
- Held sideways, pinch out to fill the screen (the picture's edges are cropped) and pinch in to
  fit it again. The subtitles stay on screen. In portrait the picture always fits; filling lasts
  until you close the player, so the next episodes play filled too.

**On a TV:** with your iPhone or iPad mirrored to a TV over AirPlay (Control Center → Screen
Mirroring), or connected to a monitor with a cable, the video plays full screen on the TV and your
device shows "Playing on the Other Screen" where the picture was, with the controls and the Japanese
lines to read, tap and look up there. Stop mirroring and the picture comes back.

**AirPlay to a TV:** pick an Apple TV (or another AirPlay TV) with the **AirPlay** button in the
player's top bar and the episode plays on the TV, from the same moment, at the TV's quality. Your
device shows "Playing on" and the TV's name where the picture was, and keeps the controls and the
Japanese lines to read, tap and look up; play, pause, seeking, the speed, Condensed and Pause After
Each Line all steer the TV. Pick your iPhone or iPad again and the picture comes back where the TV
got to. Some things work differently there:
- The TV shows Jellyfin's Japanese subtitles (with English to pick on the TV, off unless you turn
  it on). A Jimaku or Kaku file that isn't on Jellyfin can't go along, and a timing fix you haven't
  saved to Jellyfin isn't used: the player says so once.
- The TV fetches the video from Jellyfin itself, so it has to reach the server. If it can't, the
  player says so and AirPlay plays the sound only, with the picture on your device.
- A downloaded MKV file plays only the sound over AirPlay (AirPlay can't play MKV).
- With Condensed on, the TV may pause briefly at its jumps: a far jump restarts Jellyfin's stream
  there.

An AirPlay speaker gets the sound, as before. Screen Mirroring works as above.

**Picture in Picture** (iPhone and iPad): choose it in the sliders menu and the episode carries on
in a small window while you use other apps; the player shows "Playing in Picture in Picture".
**Back Here**, or the small window's button, brings it back to the player at the same moment. Closing
the small window with its × stops there, paused. The small window is iOS's own player, so it plays
the episode as Jellyfin sends it, and some things stay in Miru:
- No tappable lines or lookups, no Lines list and no word marks. Jellyfin's Japanese subtitles show
  as plain text (when the episode has them as text, not pictures).
- Subtitles from Jimaku or Kaku that aren't uploaded to Jellyfin aren't there: it plays without them
  and says so. A timing fix you haven't uploaded isn't applied either: the subtitles keep Jellyfin's
  timing.
- No condensed playback, auto-pause, intro skipping, English subtitles or sync changes.
- An MKV download can't go to Picture in Picture (the option isn't shown); other downloads can.

Files iOS can't play as they are (MKV, for example) are converted by Jellyfin while the small window
plays, at a quality that suits the small window (4 Mbps at most), keeping the audio track you chose.
Starting takes a few seconds while Jellyfin gets ready.

On the Mac, click to show the controls and double-click for full screen. The video plays in the main
window; Escape closes it.

When an episode ends, an **Up Next** card counts down to the next one (pause to hold it, or cancel
it). After a movie or a show's last episode, Miru suggests something similar under **You might like**.

The screen stays on while a video plays, and the sound keeps going when you leave the app.

The sliders button at the top of the player has the **speed** (kept for the next video), the **audio
track** when there's more than one, **English Subtitles** when the episode has them, and **Pause After
Each Line**: playback then stops at the end of every Japanese line, with the line still on screen,
until you press play (the **O** key switches it too).

**Condensed** (in the same menu, or the **C** key) skips the stretches with no Japanese line: when
more than 3 seconds pass between lines, playback jumps to half a second before the next one. The
opening before the first line is skipped too; the ending after the last line isn't. It's kept per
show, it's off until you turn it on, and it needs Japanese subtitles. With it on, the slider darkens
the stretches it skips. Progress and Stats work as usual (Stats counts only what played).

The same button has **Subtitles** (their **Size**, their **Position**, higher to clear subtitles burnt
into the picture, and **Hide Until Tapped**, which blurs each Japanese line until you tap it so you
listen first) and a **Sleep Timer** (15 to 60 minutes, or the end of the episode, which also holds
back the next one). On the Mac, **Pause Under the Pointer** stops the video while the pointer rests on
the line. These are also in **Settings → Subtitles**.

Hold a finger on the picture to play at **2×** until you let go.

Touch and hold an episode (or right-click it) to **Mark as Watched** or **Mark as Unwatched**: for
something you watched elsewhere, or to send a sampled episode back to the start and out of Continue
Watching.

If the video stops to wait for the network, Miru shows **Buffering** with how far the refill is. When
it keeps happening Miru gathers more before carrying on (up to 20 seconds of video), so a slow
connection gives a few longer waits instead of stopping every few seconds, and it shows how fast the
video is arriving next to what it needs.

**Quality.** Miru plays the original file unless you choose a lower **Streaming Quality** (8, 4, 2, 1
or 0.5 Mbps) in **Settings → Playback** or the player's sliders menu. Jellyfin then makes a smaller
stream for any file larger than that, which is what a slow connection needs; changing it while
watching carries on from the same place. When the connection can't keep up, the buffering box offers
the quality that fits it in one tap. Your subtitles, lookups and cards work the same (cards still
take their picture and audio from the original), but the audio menu has only the track being played:
Japanese when the file has it. Downloads always play as they are. With
a keyboard, **I** shows the stream's details, including how often and how long it has waited.

### Tapping subtitles

Japanese subtitles show as text you can tap. Tapping a word pauses the video and opens the
dictionary popup; closing it plays again. The popup's **Add to Anki** makes a card with:

- the subtitle line as the sentence,
- the line's audio, cut from the video,
- a picture: an **Animated** GIF of the line (the default) or a **Screenshot**, chosen under
  **Card Picture** in **Settings → Anki**,
- the show and episode, and the time.

Words you haven't mined yet, and words you're still learning, are underlined (see
[word marks](/lookup-and-anki/)). If the episode has English subtitles too, they can show at the top
of the picture (the **E** key on a keyboard).

### Skipping intros

When Jellyfin knows where an episode's intro, recap, credits or preview is (for example from an
intro-detection plugin on your server), a **Skip Intro** (or Skip Recap, Skip Credits…) button
appears at that moment. To skip intros and recaps without the button, turn on **Skip Intros
Automatically** in **Settings → Playback**.

### Chapters

When a video has chapters (from its file, as Jellyfin lists them, or a downloaded file's own),
small marks on the playback bar show where each one starts, and scrubbing names the chapter
you're in. **Previous Chapter** and **Next Chapter** are in the playback options menu (the sliders
button), and on **Page Up** and **Page Down** with a keyboard; a few seconds into a chapter,
Previous Chapter goes back to its start.

### Now Playing and AirPods

Miru shows on the Lock Screen and in Control Center, where you can pause and scrub. With AirPods:

- **Press** to play or pause.
- **Double-press** to go to the next line.
- **Triple-press** to go back to the line before the one on screen, to hear it again.

The media keys on a Mac keyboard work too.

## Subtitles

The subtitles button in the player opens the **Subtitles** sheet: the Japanese tracks Jellyfin has,
the file you picked, and the timing.

### Jimaku

[Jimaku](https://jimaku.cc) has Japanese subtitles for most anime. Make a free account there, create
an API key on its Account page, and enter it in **Settings → Subtitles → Jimaku**. Then **Find on Jimaku…** in the
Subtitles sheet searches for the show and lists its files. (When an episode has no Japanese
subtitles at all, the player shows a **Find Subtitles** button that does the same.) The file you pick is kept on your device for that episode and wins over Jellyfin's
own tracks.

Nothing goes to your Jellyfin server unless you ask: **Save to Jellyfin…** in the Subtitles sheet
(after a confirmation) adds the file, with your timing, next to the episode on your server.

### Transcribing with Kaku

For an episode nobody has subtitled, [Kaku](/kaku/) can make Japanese subtitles from the audio. Set
up Kaku in **Settings → Kaku**, then in the Subtitles sheet choose **Transcribe with
Kaku**, or **Transcribe the Rest of the Season** for the later episodes too. The sheet shows Kaku's
progress or your place in its queue.

The result is labelled "From Kaku" and works like a Jimaku file: you can tap it, sync it, save it to
Jellyfin, and watch with it offline. When you open an episode that has no Japanese subtitles, Miru
checks Kaku and uses a finished transcript by itself.

### Fixing the timing

Subtitles from elsewhere are often a little early or late. In the Subtitles sheet's **Timing**
section:

- Nudge them by 1 second or 0.1 second at a time, or **Reset**.
- **Auto-Sync to the Audio** lines the whole file up for you: against the video's own subtitle
  tracks if it has any, or else by listening to the speech in the audio.

Retiming keeps a copy on your device; Jellyfin gets it only when you save.

## Lines

**Lines** is a history of what you've watched, with the Japanese subtitles of each episode. It
includes episodes watched in any Jellyfin app on any device, not just Miru.

- Open an episode to read its lines. Tap a word to look it up, or use **Play from Here** to jump into
  the video at that line.
- **Search** finds a word or phrase across every saved episode; inside an episode, search just that
  one.
- **Watching Now** shows what another device is playing from your libraries, with its subtitles
  following along live. Tap words as they go by, scroll back, and **Follow** to catch up. You can
  also pause and resume that device from here. If Jellyfin stops answering for a moment, the list
  stays (with a note) and clears only after about 30 seconds; if the subtitles can't be loaded,
  **Try Again**.

## Keyboard shortcuts

With a keyboard on the Mac or iPad, most of mpv's familiar keys work: Space to pause, ← and → to
seek, ↑ and ↓ for the previous and next line, **[** and **]** for speed, **Z** and **X** to move the
subtitles, **S** to skip an intro, **Page Up** and **Page Down** for chapters, **A** to look up the current line, and more. Press **?** over the
video, or open **Settings → Keyboard Shortcuts**, for the full list. Outside the player, **⌘R**
reloads the screen you're on (the Mac has no pull to refresh); Yomu and Kiku have it too.

## Downloads

On a show's page, download single episodes or several at once. Each is the original file, often 1–2
GB, together with its subtitles. On iPhone and iPad, downloads wait for Wi-Fi unless **Download over
Cellular** is on in **Settings → Downloads**. Nothing downloads unless you ask.

Downloaded episodes play offline, with lookups and mining. Manage them in **Settings → Storage**,
which also holds the saved subtitles Lines uses.

## Progress sync

While you watch, Miru reports to Jellyfin as any Jellyfin app does: your place, pauses and the
episode you're on, so Jellyfin's resume points and "watched" marks stay right. If you
watched further on another device meanwhile, Miru moves there when you come back. Reports made
offline are sent later.

## Stats

The **Stats** tab shows your watching time by week, month or all time, with lookups, cards mined and
your streak. Only time that actually played counts, not seeking. Your
[kechimochi](https://github.com/Morgawr/kechimochi) log can show there too (**Settings → Stats**).

## Settings

- **Watching:** Jellyfin, Libraries, Subtitles (Jimaku and Kaku), Kaku (what it's transcribing), Playback (skip intros, Japanese
  audio only), Downloads, and Keyboard Shortcuts on iPad and Mac.
- **Lookup and Mining:** dictionaries, word audio sources and Anki, including the card picture. See
  [Lookup and Anki](/lookup-and-anki/).
- **Stats:** the kechimochi address, and **Sync with iCloud** (on unless you turn it off), which keeps
  the Stats tab and the player's settings the same on all your devices signed in to the same iCloud
  account: speed, subtitle size and position, hiding the line until tapped, pausing after lines or
  on hover, the card picture and skipping intros (the latest change wins), the libraries you've hidden
  or marked Japanese only, a Jimaku subtitle's timing fix (where the same file is on the other device)
  and the kechimochi address. Stream quality stays on each device, since it depends on that device's
  connection. When this app shares its setup with your other apps (and no Hachidori is linked), the lookup
  popup's settings are kept the same too.
- **Storage**, **Advanced** (sharing the setup with your other apps, things waiting to sync, Run
  Setup Again, Export Logs) and **About**.
