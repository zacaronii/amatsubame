Hiku is a Safari extension. Turn it on, tap Japanese text on any web page, and you get the same
dictionary popup as in Yomu, Miru and Kiku, with **Add to Anki** right there. It works in Safari on
iPhone, iPad and Mac.

Hiku comes as a small app with the extension inside it. The app is where you check that the
extension is on and, when your apps share their setup, where its dictionaries and Anki settings live.

## Turning it on

Install Hiku, then turn the extension on in Safari's settings.

On iPhone and iPad:

1. Open the Settings app, then **Apps → Safari → Extensions**.
2. Tap **Hiku** and turn it on.
3. Under **Permissions**, allow it on all websites (or the ones you read).

On the Mac:

1. Open **Safari → Settings → Extensions**.
2. Turn on **Hiku** and allow it on the websites you read.

The Hiku app shows whether the extension is on. If it's off, **Open Safari Settings** takes you
straight to the right place (on iOS 26.2 and later, and on the Mac). The same steps are in the app
under **How to Turn It On**.

## Looking words up

Tap Japanese text on a page and the popup opens beside it, for the word that starts where you
tapped. Everything you know from the other apps is there: definitions from your dictionaries,
readings, pitch accent, frequency, and word audio. See [Lookup and Anki](/lookup-and-anki/) for
what's in the popup.

- While the popup is open, a tap anywhere on the page closes it (even on a link, so you don't follow
  it by accident).
- **Add to Anki** makes a card with the sentence from the page. For a few seconds you can **Undo**
  it; then it goes to Anki.

### The extension's menu

Open Hiku from Safari's extensions menu (the puzzle piece in the address bar) for its settings:

- **Tap to look up:** turns tapping on or off.
- **Japanese text only** (on by default): taps on English, numbers and punctuation look nothing up,
  so ordinary taps on a page still work as usual.
- Which dictionaries the extension is using.
- **Anki:** when Hiku keeps its own setup, this is where you enter your AnkiConnect address, the API
  key if you use one, and the deck, then **Check and Save**. When the apps share their setup, it
  says AnkiConnect is set in the Hiku app instead.

## Word marks

If you mine to Anki, Hiku underlines words on web pages the way Yomu marks its books: words not yet
in your mining deck get a line in Hiku's colour, words you're still learning a dotted grey line, and
words you know stay plain. The marks are drawn over the page, so the page itself isn't changed. The
**Mark Words by Anki State** setting, under Lookup and Mining, turns them off. More about word
marks in [Lookup and Anki](/lookup-and-anki/).

## The Hiku app

What the Hiku app shows depends on whether your apps share their setup (see
[Getting started](/getting-started/)):

- **Shared setup:** Hiku uses the dictionaries, word audio sources and AnkiConnect settings you set
  up in Yomu, Miru or Kiku, and its app has the same **Lookup and Mining** settings as theirs, plus
  **Setup** (shared, or Hiku's own) and **About**. Change something here and it changes in the other
  apps too.
- **Its own setup:** the extension uses the dictionaries built into it, and you set up Anki in the
  extension's menu in Safari. The app then only shows the extension's status, the steps to turn it
  on, and its version.

## Settings

- **Safari:** whether the extension is on, **How to Turn It On** and **Use It in Safari**.
- **Lookup and Mining** (shared setup): dictionaries, word audio sources and Anki. See
  [Lookup and Anki](/lookup-and-anki/).
- **Setup** and **About**.
- In Safari, the extension's own menu: **Tap to look up**, **Japanese text only**, and Anki when
  Hiku keeps its own setup.
