# Yomu (the reader): working notes

Target and scheme `Yomu` (folder `Yomu/`, tests `YomuTests` and `YomuUITests`), `Yomu.app`, bundle `com.amatsubame.yomu`: Kavita, the manga and EPUB readers, OCR and read-along. Read this when working on Yomu. Shared notes are in [shared.md](shared.md).

- Yomu UI tests (live Kavita, read-only; skipped without the env vars): pass `TEST_RUNNER_UITEST_KAVITA_URL` and `TEST_RUNNER_UITEST_KAVITA_KEY` (values from your gitignored `.env`, read literally, not `source`d, since keys and passwords may contain shell characters) to `xcodebuild test -scheme Yomu -destination '<iPhone or iPad simulator>' -only-testing:YomuUITests`
- OCR models for tests: `scripts/fetch-ocr-models.sh` (into gitignored `Models/`), then `OCR_MODELS_DIR=…/Models swift test` in `Packages/OCR`; add `OCR_PAGES_DIR=<folder of page images>` for the real-page report (writes annotated images; never commit pages).
- OCR UI tests also need `TEST_RUNNER_UITEST_OCR_MODELS=<folder containing compiled/>` plus `TEST_RUNNER_UITEST_DICTIONARY_ZIPS`, `TEST_RUNNER_UITEST_LIBRARY` and `TEST_RUNNER_UITEST_SERIES` (a Japanese series).
- Book UI tests (`BookUITests`): add `TEST_RUNNER_UITEST_BOOK_LIBRARY` and `TEST_RUNNER_UITEST_BOOK_SERIES` (a library and an EPUB series in it) to the Kavita env; `TEST_RUNNER_UITEST_DICTIONARY_ZIPS` enables the lookup test.
- Book reader JS can be checked outside the app with a small WKWebView harness that injects `book.css`, `TextTapBridge.js` and `BookReader.js` into an unpacked chapter (off-screen views don't run animation frames, so don't rely on rAF).
- Reader debug launch options: `READER_DOWNLOAD_CHAPTERS=<chapterId>:<seriesId>,…` queues those downloads (from the library cache); `READER_REMOVE_DOWNLOADS=<chapterId>,…` or `=automatic` removes them (cleanup after live checks).
- Page turning:
  - **How the pager is built:** it's an eager HStack; pages more than `PagedReaderView.builtAround` (2) from the current one are placeholders.
  - **Zoom view:** its pan is off at 1×.
  - **Decoded pages:** kept in `PageImageView.decoded`, and the spinner waits 0.3 s.
  - **Test:** `testTurningPatternsAlwaysLandOnAPage`, needing the Kavita env plus `TEST_RUNNER_UITEST_DECODE_DELAY_MS=150` for device-like decoding. Debug builds add "aligned" or "part-way" to the reader's value.
  - **Recording:** record the simulator (`xcrun simctl io <udid> recordVideo`) and check for black flashes with `swift scripts/analyze-page-turns.swift <mov>`. The pinch step and the reader opening show up as expected black frames.
- **Book progress in characters:** Kavita's figures are wrong for Japanese books.
  - **Why:** its word count is letters ÷ 5 (Kavita #4805, discussion #4809), and its pages are sections of very different lengths.
  - **How Yomu counts:** every book number comes from Yomu's own counts, done Hoshi Reader's and ッツ's way: letters only (kana, kanji, ー, 々, Latin, full-width and Korean letters and digits), without punctuation, spaces or furigana. The code is `BookTextIndex.count(html:)` and the `countedCharacter` class in `BookReader.js`. Counts match Hoshi Reader's for the same book.
  - **Where counts are kept:** each EPUB volume's count per section goes in `bookLength` (Core `v27_book_length`, `BookLengths`), saved whenever a book opens or by `BookLengthCounter`.
  - **The counter:** it counts from Kavita's `GET /api/Book/{chapterId}/book-page?page=N` (a section's HTML, no images, 0-based like the spine; fetching doesn't change progress), keeps only the numbers, and is started from Settings ▸ Storage ▸ Character Counts.
    - **What it counts:** light novel libraries in full, plus volumes Kavita tags `ja` in other book libraries.
    - **After the first count:** new or changed volumes of counted series are counted after a library refresh on unmetered networks, and a series page counts its own missing volumes. A volume counts as changed when Kavita's pages or word count changed.
  - **Display:** series tiles, the series page, volume bars and rings, and Continue Reading use `BookCharacters` (in `ChapterNaming.swift`). A volume not counted yet is estimated from Kavita's word count × 5, shown with "~".
  - **What Kavita is still used for:** only the place between devices (section and paragraph). A section from another device counts as everything before it until the volume is opened here.
  - **Saved places:** they were cleared in `v26_counted_letters`, so each opens once from its xpath and then saves in the new count.
- **Read-along** (milestone 5, docs/AUDIO_PLAN.md): an EPUB volume paired with an Audiobookshelf audiobook plays with the text.
  - **Servers:** Settings ▸ Servers has Audiobookshelf and Kaku (AppCommon's `AudiobookshelfSettingsView`/`KakuSettingsView`, signed in separately from Kiku: servers are per app). Yomu's outbox also sends `AudiobookshelfProgressHandler` jobs, and read-along listening goes to Audiobookshelf as listening sessions from "Yomu" (`AudiobookshelfSessionHandler`, the shared `ListeningSessionReporter`; docs/kiku.md ▸ Listening sessions).
  - **Pairing** (`Yomu/ReadAlong/ReadAlongViews.swift`): the series page of an EPUB series has a Read-Along section; a volume is paired with a book (suggested by title), stored in `ReadAlongStore` (Core `v23_read_along`).
  - **Matching, on the device:** `BookTextCollector` loads each spine item once in a hidden `BookWebController` and keeps `BookReader.js` `wordRuns()` (cached as `.read-along-runs-v1.json` in the book folder), so positions are (section, run, UTF-16 offset), the same as word marks. `ReadAlongAligner` matches them with Kaku's transcript (unique 6-character anchors, the longest chain in order, interpolation; kana and width folded) into `ReadAlongCues`, saved with the pair. On the novel it was tested with, 82% of the book's sentences matched (front matter, contents and the afterword aren't narrated), in about a minute in the simulator.
  - **In the reader:** `ReadAlongModel` (an `AudioPlayer` on the stream or Yomu's own download in `Application Support/ReadAlongAudio`, made by `ReadAlongDownloads`: owned by `AppEnvironment`, one task per book with progress from the transfer's byte counts and Cancel; Delete, Settings ▸ Storage ▸ Remove (asked first) and Unpair (unless another volume pairs the same book) stop a running download and wait for it before deleting; a problem loading the audio shows **Try Again**, `ReadAlongModel.retry`) highlights the spoken sentence with `bookReader.readAlong.show` (a `<km-ra>` wrapper, like `<km-w>`) and turns the page or opens the next section to follow; paging or scrolling by hand (playing or paused) stops following and shows Follow Audio; playing again follows. The audio bar comes and goes with the reader's controls (a tap in the middle of the page); Follow Audio and From Here show either way. The headphones button in the reader's top bar turns read-along off for the volume (plain reading: no audio, highlight or Follow Audio; `ReadAlongPair.readsAlong`, Core `v24_read_along_off`) and on again from the page you're on; Kiku's Read in Yomu turns it back on. Turning it on from the switch listens from the page on screen: the last matched sentence at or before its first character (`firstVisiblePlace`, `ReadAlongCues.startIndex`). Reopening a book on the same device uses its own place to the character (`LocalReadingProgress.characters`; `BookReader.js` `charactersBeforePage`/`pageOfCharacter`), since a page can start inside a paragraph whose xpath (Kavita's position) is a page earlier. Hiding the status bar with the controls doesn't move the text (`BookReaderModel.setSafeArea` keeps the larger inset until the screen size changes). A lookup pauses and offers From Here, with the sentence's clip above the popup (`ReadAlongClipRow`, like Kiku's); its card gets the sentence's audio (cut from the download, or Kaku's `/clips` while streaming). Kavita progress follows the turned pages as usual; Audiobookshelf's goes through the outbox. On opening, if Audiobookshelf moved after the volume was last read (listening in Kiku), the book opens at that sentence.
  - **The bar** sits in the reader's stack (`BookReaderView`'s accessory), not an overlay, and its buttons and capsule have tap shapes: otherwise taps went through to the page and toggled the controls.
  - **From Kiku** (milestone 6): Yomu handles `amatsubame-yomu://read-along?item=<Audiobookshelf item>&t=<seconds>` (`AppEnvironment.open(_:)`, `CFBundleURLTypes` in `Yomu/Resources/Info.plist`). It finds the paired volume (`ReadAlongStore.pairs(itemId:)`), builds the session from the library cache (`KavitaLibraryRepository.cachedSeriesId(chapterId:)`), and hands the time to read-along (`linkedListening`; saving it as listening progress didn't work, because the reader closing saves its own place over it). A reader already showing switches in place (`requestedReader`); otherwise the root presents it. An unpaired book gets an alert.
  - **Screen:** while read-along plays, the screen doesn't dim (`ScreenAwake`, AppCommon; also Miru's playback).
  - **Live test:** `ReadAlongUITests` (in memory, nothing sent; `testReaderKeepsItsPlace` checks the page holds through the controls, the switch and a reopen) needs the Kavita env plus `TEST_RUNNER_UITEST_ABS_URL/KEY`, `TEST_RUNNER_UITEST_KAKU_URL/TOKEN`, `TEST_RUNNER_UITEST_READALONG_LIBRARY` and `_SERIES` (a Kavita library and an EPUB series paired with an Audiobookshelf audiobook), `_ITEM` (the audiobook's id) for the link check, and optionally `_CHARACTERS` (the book's character count as shown, e.g. `12,345`) to check the count exactly. The link check opens it through `XCUIDevice.shared.system.open` (`app.open` relaunches the app and loses its in-memory data). Queries on a book page are slow (its text is in the accessibility tree), about 20 s each.

- **Small conveniences:** the screen stays on while the reader is open (`ScreenAwake` counts its reasons, so read-along pausing doesn't undo it), until 15 minutes pass without the chapter's local progress moving (`ReaderContainer.staysAwake`). A library's grid on iPhone and iPad has the Mac's filter and sort as a menu (`libraryMenu`, AppCommon): Unread, Finished and the sort, beside the All · Reading · Downloaded picker (`LibraryFilterUITests` opens it against a real Kavita: `TEST_RUNNER_UITEST_LIBRARY`).
- **Whose place a chapter is at, one rule** (`LocalReadingProgress.devicesPlaceWins`, used by the series page via `KavitaSeriesContents.readingFurther`, a reading list via `ReadingListEntry`, and the readers' start via `ProgressSyncPolicy.startPosition`): this device's progress row wins outright while it has a change for the chapter or its series not sent yet (`OutboxStore.unsentProgressKeys`: waiting, backing off, held by `OutboxWait`, or being sent), so what was read here offline is shown while it waits; whether it reaches Kavita is still the sender's call by when it was made (#204's clock limits stand). Otherwise the newer of the two wins outright, so a chapter marked unread (or read less far) on another device or in Kavita's web reader shows here too (Kavita's unread moves the date both `Series/volumes` and a list's items carry, checked live 2026-11-23). A change that failed for good no longer protects the row (dates decide). Kavita's count without a date (an older cache) is weighed as before on the screens (the larger of the two), while the reader takes this device's place then. At a reader's start (manga and books), a queued change protects this device's place only if the sender would still send it, judged against Kavita's progress as just read with the sender's own check (`KavitaProgressCheck.queuedChangeStillGoes` / `stillGoes`, shared with `KavitaProgressHandler`): a stuck change over newer reading elsewhere would be dropped, so the reader opens at Kavita's place. The series page (`ReadingProgressStore.observePlaces`) and a list (`observeEntries`) observe the progress rows and the queue together and recompute against Kavita's side as last read whenever either changes; they read Kavita again on their own schedule (the series page: on appear, on pull to refresh, and a second after the reader closes; a list: when its screen opens), so after a change is skipped for newer reading elsewhere, Kavita's newer place shows at that next read. Builds that hold page turns (DevSetup's held progress, UI tests) keep them queued, so there this device's place always wins; Yomu has no user setting that stops page turns being sent. Known limits: the screens keep plain `unsent` (the sender's check needs the payloads, which Core doesn't have), so for a stuck change over newer reading elsewhere they show this device's place until the change is dropped and Kavita is next read, while the reader opens at Kavita's; on a device whose clock is behind, a change shows while queued, and once sent the screen can fall back to Kavita's older cached place until that next read (Kavita itself holds what was sent).
- **Page turns judged by when they were made** (#204): `ReadingProgressRecorder` stamps each queued turn with the time the page was turned (`KavitaProgressPayload.madeAt`, the device row's time; kept in the job, never sent), and `KavitaProgressHandler` skips a turn if Kavita's progress for the chapter changed after that (read elsewhere meanwhile). Kavita's newer date can also be this device's own previous turn, still on its way when the next was made: it's recognised by Kavita holding exactly what this device last sent for the chapter or its series (`OutboxStore.lastSent`, the later of the chapter's and the series' sent jobs; sent progress jobs are kept 7 days). Kavita gives back exactly what was sent (`pageNum` and `bookScrollId`, checked live on the test account 2026-11-23); a read mark leaves the chapter's pages as pages read (the book's place kept), so marks carry each chapter's pages (`chapterPages`) and a read mark counts only when Kavita holds exactly that; an unread mark when it holds 0; a mark from an older build, without pages, never. Chapter marks use the same check. Jobs queued before this have no `madeAt` and are judged by when they're sent, as before. Known limits: a device whose clock is behind stamps turns early, so if the same chapter was read elsewhere within that difference, an offline turn made after it can be skipped; one whose clock is ahead stamps them late, so reading done elsewhere within that difference after the turn is overwritten (Kavita's own date isn't compared with the device's clock anywhere else); a turn whose request reached Kavita but whose answer was lost stays queued, and a turn made meanwhile is folded into it and then finds Kavita holding a place no sent job has, so it's skipped until the next turn; a series mark's own check is by dates alone (a series mark made while a change to one of its chapters is on its way is dropped).
- **Mark as Read / Unread** (the series page's volume and chapter menus, and its ⋯ menu for the whole series, asked first): `AppEnvironment.setRead` → `KavitaReadMarking` saves this device's place and queues a `KavitaReadMarkPayload` in the progress lane (`kavitaProgress`) in one write: one job per chapter with the chapter's coalesce key (the latest of a mark and a page turn is what's sent), or one `kavita.series.N` job that cancels the series' queued chapter jobs. `KavitaProgressHandler` sends marks through Kavita's mark endpoints (`mark-multiple-read/unread` with `chapterIds`, `mark-read/unread` for a series, all in 0.7.8), never `Reader/progress`, which has fed Kavita's reading sessions since 0.8.9 (#183's marks showed there as pages read; Kavita has no route to remove them). A mark carries the time it was made (`madeAt`, the progress rows' time) and is judged by it, not by when its job is sent: a chapter mark reads the chapter's progress, a series mark reads `Series/volumes` once (specials and loose chapters included) and is dropped whole if any chapter changed on Kavita after it was made; dropped marks end quietly and the device follows Kavita at the next refresh. After an unread mark goes, the handler reads Kavita's date back and moves this device's rows the mark wrote (still page 0 at `madeAt`) just past it (`KavitaReadMarkSettling`), so this device starts over at the top. That read-back is best effort: if it fails, the mark still counts as sent (a retry would only find its own newer date and end), the row isn't moved, and this device follows Kavita's kept place for that book. A mark made under another account waits (`OutboxWait`). Marking writes no `activitySession` (no time, characters or streak). Known limits: Kavita's unread keeps a book's `bookScrollId`, so on other devices the book reopens at page 0 where that XPath resolves in the first section, else at its top (paged: the page holding it; scrolling: scrolled to it); a series mark made before this device's previous change to the series reached Kavita is judged against that change and dropped (chapter marks and page turns tell this device's own last send apart, see below); marks queued by builds before this went out once as plain progress; plain page turns carry no account. The page shows it at once (`KavitaSeriesContents.settingPagesRead`). When the page next loads, this device's place wins over Kavita's while it's the newer of the two (`readingFurther`, by the one rule above), so an unread mark doesn't bounce back before Kavita has it. **Time left in the chapter** (books): `BookTextIndex.charactersLeftInChapter` counts to where the next contents entry starts, shown before the book's time left.
- **Which character a tap hits (manga):** the recognizer gives a block's text and box, not character positions, so `TextLines.layout` measures them in the block's picture (a little more than the box, Otsu threshold, up to 1000 px; small blocks on low-resolution pages are doubled) and `OCRBlock.place` keeps the result (`lines`, `cells`: one rect per `Character` in the block's unit square, `cellsExact`, `measured` = `TextLines.version`; pages measured by an older version are measured again from the cached text when opened, no models needed).
  - **Ink:** only the lettering's: ink that runs off the picture's edge (the bubble's outline, art) and ink that doesn't touch the largest stretch of paper (screen tone past the outline in the corners) are cleared (`clearEdges`).
  - **Lines:** a projection across the lines. A full line's thickness comes from the runs' widths; runs thicker than that are parted at valleys (furigana stuck to its line, lines that touch), pieces of one line are joined, and a thin run within reach of a full line on its furigana side is furigana, not a line. Thin unbroken strokes and furigana lying past a line's end are left out.
  - **The grid:** Japanese lettering is set one character to a square, so one pitch is fitted for the whole block (each line with its own start): the pitch whose cuts cross the least ink (`grid`). If the pitch says the lines are thinner than first measured (furigana stuck to all of them), the lines are found again with it.
  - **Matching the text** (`placed`): a character per square, except what's drawn otherwise: a run of dots read as `．．．` is one … (or two) in a square, or a dot per square when the squares hold little ink; `！？` or two digits may share a square in a column (side by side); a long dash may take two. If the text fits the squares, the places are **exact**. If it fits once one stray line is left out (a sweat drop, a piece of outline), that line is dropped. Otherwise the places are a guess (the nearest squares, or for lettering not on a grid, Latin letters and hand-drawn titles, the glyphs by their gaps: `cellsByGaps`, exact only when every line has as many glyphs as characters).
  - **Use:** a tap goes to the nearest place, exact or guessed (`TextLayout.characterIndex(of:in:cells:)`; with no places at all, the even spread). The looked-up word is tinted on the page (`OCRTextLayer.marks`, reader settings ▸ Mark the Word on the Page) only where the places are exact: a mark that's a guess is worse than none. It follows taps in the popup's sentence (`MangaReaderModel.textOverlay`, setting `MangaReaderSettings.showsTextOverlay`, on by default). Nothing is typeset over the art (an earlier version did; the owner rejected it: it must sit on the lettering or not be there). `ReaderUITests.testLookupMarksTheWordOnThePage` runs it on real pages with screenshots.
  - **Checking on real pages:** `OCR_MODELS_DIR=… OCR_PAGES_DIR=<folder of pages> swift test --filter RealPageOCR` in `Packages/OCR` prints each block as exact or guessed and writes `annotated-*.png` with every character's place drawn (never commit pages). On 32 pages of three series (2026-11-22): 189 of 200 blocks exact; the rest were screen tone read as dots, hand lettering and sound effects.
- **Bookmarks and highlights on Kavita** (`ReaderMarks.swift` in Core, `KavitaMarks.swift` and `KavitaReaderMarkHandler.swift` in KavitaKit): every mark lives on Kavita; `readerMark` (v56) is the device's copy per account (`ReaderMarkStore.accountKey`) plus what's unsent (`state`: synced / pendingCreate / pendingUpdate / pendingDelete, `revision`). Kinds: manga page bookmarks (Kavita's `Reader/bookmark`, needs its Bookmark role; Kavita copies the page image into its bookmarks folder), book bookmarks (its personal table of contents, keyed by page and title) and highlights with notes (`Annotation`, Kavita 0.8.8+, five colour slots). Endpoint notes are at the top of `KavitaMarks.swift`.
  - **Sending:** each change writes the row and queues `kavitaReaderMark` with only the row's id (coalesced per mark) in the same write (`OutboxStore.enqueue(_:kind:coalesceKey:in:)`, so a refresh never sees a pending row without its job), so the handler sends the row as it is then: edits fold into an unsent make, a mark removed before it was sent never reaches Kavita, an edit that raced a make goes next (`ReaderMarkStore.didSend` compares `revision`). Kavita answers 400 (not 404) for an update or delete of an annotation that's gone; the handler checks `GET Annotation/{id}` first (and again after a 400): 404 means removed elsewhere, so an update makes it again once and a delete is done (a 404 on the delete itself is done too). A book bookmark made again while Kavita still has it gets "Duplicate bookmark entry already exists" (400): found in the list, it counts as made. Page bookmarks accept a repeat. A 404 on a highlight write is only "no annotations" if the list route says so; if the list can't be reached the job is tried again.
  - **What a server can't do** (`kavitaFeature`): a 404 from the annotation list (before 0.8.8) or a refusal of page bookmarks for an account without the Bookmark role marks the feature unavailable for that account, so nothing waits to sync for ever; unsent page bookmarks go (nothing typed), unsent highlights stay (below); the reader and the series page stop offering it and say why.
  - **Refreshing** (`KavitaReaderMarkSync`, when a series page loads and when the manga reader opens): page bookmarks by series, annotations by series (only this user's: the lists include other users' when they share theirs and this user views them), book bookmarks per volume. `ReaderMarkMerge.plan` applies the server's list (a change whose job was cleared in Waiting to Sync counts as synced, so the server's copy wins for it): its copy replaces synced rows, a synced row it lacks was removed elsewhere, unsent changes stay, an edit to a mark removed elsewhere makes it again, and a page bookmarked on two devices is one.
  - **Who sees annotations** is the user's own Kavita setting (Share Annotations, off by default); Yomu sends nothing about it and leaves the spoiler flag off.
  - **UI:** the manga reader's bookmark button (`PageBookmarks`, `reader.bookmark`) and the series page's Bookmarks & Highlights (`ReaderMarksSection`: tap opens the reader at the page, and in a book its place, via `ReaderSession.startPage`/`startXPath`, hold to remove). Books (`BookMarks`, `HighlightBar`): the bar above the lookup popup highlights the tapped word's sentence in one of Kavita's five colour slots, writes a note (`NoteEditor`; sent as `commentHtml` paragraphs) or removes it; the book bookmark button in the controls keeps the place on screen (`currentXPath`, titled by its first words, unique per page since Kavita deletes by page and title). In `BookReader.js`: `sentenceAt`/`markAt` read the sentence and highlight at the tapped point, captured when the tap arrives (the section can lay out again while the popup opens, and the point would then be elsewhere); `showUserMarks` wraps each highlight in `<km-hl>` by finding its text (whitespace ignored, furigana skipped) between its start and end elements, drawn as boxes outside the body in paged layouts like read-along (`placeUserMarks`); the start and end elements skip the reader's own wrappers so the XPaths are Kavita's. `BookMarksScriptTests` (macOS web view) covers the script.
  - **Live UI tests:** `ReaderUITests.testPageBookmarkReachesKavitaAndOpensFromTheSeries` and `BookUITests.testHighlightNoteAndBookmarkReachKavita` run against the test account with `UITEST_SENDS_MARKS` (marks are sent, progress isn't). `KavitaTestAccount` (YomuUITests) takes a snapshot of the account's annotations, page bookmarks and the book's bookmarks first, and teardown removes whatever was added, so a run that fails halfway leaves nothing. With `DEV_HOLD_PROGRESS` marks stay queued too.
  - **Opening from a mark** (the series list) starts the reader at its page (and in a book, its XPath) without recording progress: only a page turn does, so closing again leaves the reading position as it was, on the device and on Kavita.
  - **Notes and Kavita's formatting:** an update is sent over Kavita's current copy (fetched first), so a colour change keeps a note formatted in Kavita's web reader; changing the note's text in Yomu replaces it with plain paragraphs. An empty note (spaces only) is no note.
  - **Known limit:** a highlight is found again by its text between its start and end elements; if the same text occurs twice there, the first is marked.
  - **Signing in** to a server (any app, `ServerSettingsModel.saveAccount`) makes what waited for it due at once, rather than after its backoff (marks of another Kavita account wait while it's signed out).
  - **Unavailable annotations keep notes:** a server found without annotations keeps unsent highlights as rows without jobs (shown "Not on Kavita yet"; they survive restarts and signing in again to the same account), and a refresh that finds annotations queues them again. Only a 404 from the list route decides it; a 404 on a single write is that job's failure. A mark made under one Kavita account waits while another is signed in. Bookmarking a page again before its removal was sent takes the same row back, so a retried removal can't follow the make.
- **Want to Read on Kavita** (`WantToRead.swift` in Core, `KavitaWantToRead.swift` in KavitaKit): `wantToRead` (v57) is the device's copy per account (`ReaderMarkStore.accountKey`): Kavita's list in its order (`position`) and unsent toggles (`pendingAdd`/`pendingRemove`, `revision`). A toggle saves at once and queues `kavitaWantToRead` (`WantToReadJob`, coalesced per account and series) in the same write; the handler sends only rows still `pendingAdd`/`pendingRemove` (a job run again after the app died mid-way finds its row settled), sent as `add-series`/`remove-series` (both idempotent on Kavita). While another Kavita account is signed in, a waiting toggle is neither shown nor sent: its job waits, and signing back in sends it at once.
  - **Refresh** (`KavitaWantToReadSync`, with the Library home): the whole `want-to-read/v2` list, page by page (100 a page, up to 200 pages), merged so toggles still waiting stay (a pending row whose job was cleared in Waiting to Sync, or is gone, follows Kavita again; a failed job still listed keeps it), and so do toggles Kavita took after the list was fetched (`listedAt`; a sent removal stays as a `removed` row until a later list no longer has the series); its series are cached (`KavitaLibraryRepository.remember`) so the shelf works offline. The series page asks `GET want-to-read?seriesId` when it opens, which fills in only what isn't pending.
  - **Older Kavita:** the list route is in 0.7.8 and later (checked against the release tags; add, remove and the check are in 0.7.5). Its 404 marks `kavitaFeature.wantToRead` unavailable and drops unsent toggles (nothing typed); a 404 on one toggle with the list answering is that job's failure, and a list that can't be reached is retried.
  - **Shelf:** after Continue Reading, filtered like it (libraries Yomu reads, none hidden in Settings, offline only downloaded series), and absent when empty. `ReaderUITests.testWantToReadReachesKavitaAndTheShelf` adds and removes on the test account through the app; `KavitaTestAccount` restores Want to Read both ways.
- **Reading lists on Kavita** (`ReadingLists.swift` in Core, `KavitaReadingLists.swift` in KavitaKit; endpoint notes at its top): `kavitaReadingList`, `kavitaReadingListItem` and `kavitaReadingListMove` (v59) per account, with the rules collections have (row and job in one write, a pending row protected only while its job is listed, `listedAt`, the handler acting only on pending rows, adoption of a waiting create by title on refresh, delete during create). Routes are in Kavita 0.7.8 (Yomu's floor anyway: its series filter API); `ownerUserName` from 0.8.8 (before it, own lists are told apart with a second `includePromoted=false` request), `itemCount` from 0.8.4 (before it the item count is unknown and the last-item note isn't shown), `provider`/`canSync` from 0.9.0 (synced lists are read-only).
  - **Items** are identified by chapter (Kavita skips a chapter already in a list). Adds are by chapter only: Add to Reading List… is in a menu that covers exactly one chapter (a chapter, or a volume of one chapter); a volume of several chapters doesn't offer it yet; taking out is in the list (swipe), and taking out an add not sent yet just cancels it. A remove finds the item's id by its chapter (read once); one gone already ends quietly.
  - **Moves** are kept as "right after this chapter", with the whole fallback chain as the list stood (`ReadingListMoveRow.anchors`, nearest first); the handler puts the item after the first anchor Kavita still has (Kavita takes the item out and inserts it at `toPosition` among the rest), sends nothing when it's already there, and waits while an add or move of the list queued before it is still waiting or being sent, so a move never overtakes one backing off (a failed one doesn't block). Waits (this one, and an add or move waiting for its list to be made) are `OutboxWait`s: decided from the device's rows before the Kavita client is made, looked at again every 30 s without counting an attempt, so a move behind a blocker in a long back-off goes within 30 s of the blocker leaving the queue however it leaves (sent, failed for good, an unsent add taken back; Clear and Delete Reading List take the move with it). A remove backing off doesn't hold a move up, and needn't: a move's anchors come from the order as shown, where the item being taken out is already gone, so the moved item is never placed after it (Kavita A B C D with B's remove waiting, the device showing A C D: D after A gives A D B C, then A D C once B goes, as shown). A move is folded into the item's previous one only when that's the list's last queued job, so the order of moves is kept. The device shows Kavita's order from the last read with the waiting moves applied; a refresh doesn't undo them. A run of N moves on a list is one read of its items (kept fresh by the moves) plus N requests. A read that skipped a row changed after it (an add sent meanwhile) leaves the items stale and keeps the count, so a remove or move of that item reads again for its Kavita id.
  - **Empty lists:** Kavita's cleanup task (daily unless the admin changes it) removes lists with no items, and taking out the last item doesn't delete one. A refresh keeps a list Kavita no longer lists when it was empty as last known and has an add still to send, leaving it to the add's job. An add that finds its list gone: empty as last known (new, or emptied from here) → made again with its waiting adds (at most twice, then a permanent failure naming it); with items as last known → deleted elsewhere, and it goes here. A create's 400 (the title exists; Kavita's message is translated, so not read) is followed by a look for the list; none found is a permanent failure naming it.
  - **Next in List** (`ReadingListStore.next`, `NextOffer` in Readers, `ReaderContainer.makeOffer`): an item opened from a list's screen sets `ReaderSession.readingListId` (no migration; the session is in memory). The end-of-volume page (manga, paged and long strip) and the book's end button then show the list's next from the device's copy (works offline): Kavita's order with waiting moves applied, unsent adds offered, items being taken out never; PDFs, image folders, hidden libraries and libraries the account can't read are passed over and counted ("skipped 1 PDF"); an item that isn't downloaded while offline is offered, marked "not on this device". The series' next is the second, smaller choice (one button when it's the same chapter); at the list's end, "End of “…”" with the series' next; the item read taken out of the list (here or by a refresh), or the list gone, falls back to "Next in series". Nothing but these buttons goes to the next chapter (swiping on past the end page stays there). A next item in the same series swaps in place as before; in another series or format the session is replaced once the series is on the device (`environment.session(chapterId:seriesId:)`), with a spinner on the button and the reason under it if it can't be fetched (the reader stays). While one choice is being fetched the other can't be pressed, a remade offer (the list changed) keeps the spinner and the reason for the same chapter (`NextOffer.carrying`), and the result is dropped unless the same chapter in the same list is still open (`ReaderContainer.Reading.isStill`; a link may have opened something else). Progress isn't touched until a page is turned. `UITEST_READER_OPENS_AT_END` (debug) opens the readers on their last page or section for the end-of-volume tests (`ReaderUITests.testTheEndOfAVolumeOffersTheListsNextAcrossFormats`, `testTheEndOfABookOffersTheListsNextManga`).
  - **Read state** on a list's screen (`ReadingListStore.entries` / `observeEntries`, `ReadingListEntry`), by the one rule below. Kavita's side comes from the list's items (`pagesRead`, `pagesTotal`, `lastReadingProgressUtc`, kept as `kavitaPagesRead/kavitaPages/kavitaReadAt`, v61, nullable, filled at the next read of the list) and is the fallback where this device has no row. The observation reads the progress rows too, so reading or Mark as Read/Unread here shows at once; a read of the list only replaces Kavita's side with a newer one, so an older read merged late can't put a mark back. Known limits: a kept reading with a date is never replaced by one without, so if Kavita ever drops a chapter's progress entirely, the list keeps the last dated value.
  - Known limits: an item that fails to open for a reason outside the skip list (offline and not downloaded, gone from Kavita before a refresh, a format Yomu doesn't know) can't be passed over from the reader: the reason shows under it, and the ways on are the series' next or the list's screen; list mode lasts while the reader is open (closing it and resuming from Continue Reading or the series is a normal read); a list's create that failed for good on a 4xx other than 400 or 404 keeps its list row, so its adds and moves are looked at every 30 s for as long as the app runs and the create is left in Waiting to Sync (cheap, shown as "Needs attention" on the create and "Waiting" on the rest; Clear removes them all). Before Kavita 0.8.4 (count unknown) an emptied list cleanup removed is taken as deleted elsewhere and its waiting add is dropped; a move on a list deleted elsewhere within the 60 s the items count as fresh gets Kavita's 400 and a permanent failure; an add taken out here while its request is on its way comes back at the next read.
  - `ReaderUITests.testReadingListReachesKavitaAndIsReorderedAndDeleted` makes a list from a volume's menu, adds a second volume, opens an item, reorders it from the Library home's list (Kavita follows), takes both out (the list stays) and deletes it, and checks no reading progress changed; `KavitaTestAccount` deletes lists the test made and fails if one that was there changed.
- **Collections on Kavita** (`Collections.swift` in Core, `KavitaCollections.swift` in KavitaKit; endpoint notes at its top): `kavitaCollection` and `kavitaCollectionSeries` (v58) per account, with the rules Want to Read has (row and job in one write, a pending row protected only while its job is listed, `listedAt`, the handler acting only on pending rows, another account's jobs waiting). Collections from Kavita 0.8.0 (the list route's own 404 hides them).
  - **New Collection** is made in two steps: Kavita makes an empty one, then the series goes in (this nightly 500s on a collection made with series). The create job first looks for an own collection with the title (Kavita's `ToNormalized`: letters, ASCII digits and `+!＊！＋`, lowercased; `CollectionRow.normalized`) that this device doesn't know yet, and takes it without posting, so a create whose answer was lost isn't made twice. Kavita doesn't refuse duplicate titles itself; the New Collection field does (trimmed, not empty, not an own title). A refused create (4xx, or 500) drops the collection with its series and their jobs, with a message naming it; outages are retried.
  - **Kavita's cleanup removes empty collections** (in 0.8.0, 0.9.1.4 and the nightly). An add that finds its collection gone (400 "collection-doesnt-exists", then not in the list): a collection made here with no series confirmed yet is made again (in the same write as its create job, at most twice; then a permanent failure naming it); one that had a series was deleted elsewhere, and the add goes. An add whose collection is gone here, being deleted, or has no id with no create listed finishes without sending.
  - **Adds before removes:** a remove first sends the same collection's waiting adds, since taking out what looks like the last series deletes the collection.
  - **The last series** is judged on the whole collection (`CollectionStore.seriesCount`): Kavita's `itemCount` (from the list or a full read of its series, and kept up to date with the adds and removes Kavita takes from here), plus the unsent changes counted against what Kavita has (`inKavita` on each series row, so a series added and taken out again before sending changes nothing). `CollectionStore.takeOut` answers not the last, the last, or can't tell (the series read earlier no longer add up to Kavita's count). The last: Yomu asks, and confirming "Take Out and Delete" deletes the collection (it leaves the device at once; an unsent New Collection is cancelled). Can't tell: Yomu asks without claiming it's the last ("This may be its last series…", button "Take Out"), and confirming only takes the series out; the handler's look afterwards drops the collection if Kavita deleted it. After a remove Kavita took, when the count says the collection is empty (or can't tell), one look at the list, and a collection gone goes from the device too, as does a confirmed collection an add finds deleted elsewhere (with its series and their jobs).
  - **Lost answers:** a refresh that lists a collection with the title of a New Collection whose create is still waiting takes it as that one (its create then sends nothing), and a create that posts and then finds the collection deleted here meanwhile deletes what it made (never one it only adopted).
  - **Opening a collection** reads its series and caches them before their ids are merged (`KavitaCollectionSync.series(of:cache:)`), so the grid, which follows the ids, finds them cached.
  - **Known limits:** a list fetched before a delete reached Kavita and merged after it shows the collection again until the next refresh (collections have no `removed` state as Want to Read rows do). A list fetched before an add or remove from here reached Kavita, and merged after it, sets `itemCount` back: on a collection never opened the count is off by one until the next refresh (it can ask once without need, or miss the question once).
  - **Read-only:** promoted collections (another `owner`) and ones with `source` ≠ 0 (AniList 1, MAL 2, CBR 4, Hardcover 5, MangaBaka 6) are refused in `CollectionStore` itself (`CollectionError.readOnly`). Delete Collection cancels its waiting adds and removes in the same write.
  - **Refreshes:** the Library home asks for the list once (`GET Collection`, own told apart by the Kavita username); a collection's series are read when it's opened (all pages, cached for the grid), and the series sheet asks `all-series?seriesId`. The grid filters like the Want to Read shelf and says how many series it doesn't show.
  - `ReaderUITests.testNewCollectionReachesKavitaAndGoesWithItsLastSeries` makes one from the series page, checks Kavita, opens it from the Library home, and takes the series out (confirmed); `KavitaTestAccount` deletes collections a test made and fails if one that was there changed.
- **Reader taps and rotation:** `ReaderTapZones` (standard, inverted, off) in both readers' settings (`MangaReaderSettings.tapZones`, `BookReaderSettings.tapZones`). Manga's Double-Tap to Zoom (`doubleTapZooms`, off by default) makes the page's tap gesture a double tap exclusively before the single one (`ZoomablePage`), zooming through `ZoomControl` into the native zoom view. Lock Rotation (`OrientationLock`, iOS; kept in UserDefaults) holds the orientation showing while a reader is open: Yomu's app delegate returns its mask. UI tests preset the taps with `UITEST_READER_TAPS`.
